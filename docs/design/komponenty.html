What it does

The parts that matter in daily use. Everything below ships in the free build — there is no other build.

  • 01 · Editor

    Drag & drop editor

    Build emails from blocks; never touch HTML. Every drag action has a keyboard equivalent.

  • 02 · AI

    AI assistant, on your own key

    Describe the campaign, get a draft. Bring your own OpenAI, Anthropic, Google or OpenRouter key — typically €0.05–0.20 per newsletter, paid to them, not us. Optional; nothing depends on it.

  • 03 · Segments

    Segments made of sentences

    No AND/OR logic trees. Pick conditions in plain language, see the live count and five real people who match.

    opened a campaign in the last 90 days → 1,204 people

  • 04 · Import

    Import that survives messy data

    Encoding and delimiter detected automatically, per-row errors you can download, fix and re-upload. Ten broken addresses don't reject a file of a thousand.

  • 05 · Deliverability

    Deliverability that protects you

    An automatic suppression list that can't be switched off, and an automatic brake: a campaign pauses itself at 8 % bounces or 0.3 % complaints, long before Amazon suspends your account. SPF, DKIM and DMARC generated with copy buttons.

  • 06 · Projects

    Twelve clients, twelve walls

    One installation, many projects. An API key belongs to exactly one project — a key for client A physically cannot read client B's contacts. Each project has its own sending account and reputation.

.wm-timeline — vlajková sekce trackingu: text + tmavý monospace log, pod tím kroky. Varianty: žádné. Volitelné části: věta pod logem (__then), blok „How the link is made“ (__how s kroky), mono poznámka (__note). Tlumený řádek události: __event--exit.

See what happens after the click.

Most tools stop at opened and clicked. Mlain Mailer links the click in the email to what the person actually did on your site — pages, products, basket — in one timeline you can read.

And then you can act on it: everyone who reached the basket and didn't buy within 24 hours gets a reminder.

How the link is made

  1. A single-use key valid for 15 minutes is attached to links in the email.
  2. Your site's script picks it up and confirms it with your server.
  3. The key is stripped from the URL immediately, so it can't be copied or shared.
  4. It is only ever added to domains you have registered yourself.

The tracking script is under 5 kB and served from your own subdomain.

One command to install. €5/month to run.

$ docker compose up -d

  • Amazon SES

    About $1 per 10,000 emails. Reports bounces and complaints back, which powers the automatic protection. Recommended above ~2,000 emails a day.

  • Your own SMTP

    The SMTP from your existing hosting works and costs effectively nothing. Recommended up to ~2,000 emails a day.

    Honest note: most SMTP providers don't report bounces and complaints back, so you lose part of the automatic protection — the interface says so too.

Any other provider speaking SMTP can be plugged in the same way.

Frequently asked questions

The questions that come up in every conversation about self-hosted email — answered without hedging.

Is it really free, including the parts other tools charge for, like automation and multiple projects?

Yes. MIT license, every feature in the public repository. No license key, no seat limit, no feature gate, no Enterprise edition.

How will you make money?

Later, from hosting, support and migrations — never from unlocking features. If we ever sell something, it will be convenience, not capability.

SES or my own SMTP — which should I use?

Your hosting's SMTP up to roughly 2,000 emails a day — it costs nothing. Above that, Amazon SES: it reports bounces and complaints back, which the automatic protection needs.

Privacy on this website

This site sets no cookies and loads nothing from third parties. There is no analytics script and no tracking pixel — the same restraint we expect from a mailing tool.

What the server logs

The web server keeps standard access logs for debugging:

  • IP address and requested URL, rotated after 14 days
  • Nothing else — no fingerprinting, no third-party requests
